## 0. Start here

This directory is for anyone building a YouTube channel, anywhere, with long-form videos, Shorts or a faceless format, and for AI agents helping them. Every fact links to its source; YouTube changes often, so check the link before acting on a number.

**The growth loop (every video):** 1 Idea (find outliers) → 2 Package (title + thumbnail) → 3 Hook (hold attention) → 4 Publish (settings, Shorts) → 5 Review (analytics) → back to 1: what worked shapes the next idea.

## 1. How recommendations work (in YouTube's own words)

YouTube does not push your video to an audience. It ranks videos separately for each viewer at the moment they open the app, aiming at long-term satisfaction. So the durable strategy is to make videos that a specific group of people choose to click and are glad they watched.

**What the system reads.** YouTube's Help Center lists its primary signals: watch history, search history, channel subscriptions, likes, dislikes, "Not interested", "Don't recommend channel" and satisfaction surveys. It learns from over 80 billion signals ([How YouTube recommendations work](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16089387)). YouTube also weighs a channel's reputation and quality, and uses external evaluators to judge how an average viewer would perceive a video ([How YouTube Works](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/recommendations/)).

**Where views come from.** Home (personalised), Up next / Suggested (beside and after a video), Search, the Shorts feed, the Subscriptions feed, notifications, channel pages and topic destination pages. Each behaves differently, and Analytics shows which one feeds each video.

**What YouTube's head of discovery said in September 2026.** Todd Beaupré (Senior Director, Growth & Discovery) on Creator Insider, as reported by [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/):

- Recommendations are built per viewer when they open YouTube; nothing is "sent" to subscribers. Even your best videos are skipped by about 90% of your subscribers, and that is normal.
- In the first hour a video is already shown to people who have never watched you. It can fail with subscribers and still win with new viewers.
- Videos are not judged only on day one. Some find most of their audience a month or more later; evergreen videos stay eligible.
- No single metric decides. YouTube does not assume all watch time is valuable, so it uses surveys to separate time spent from satisfaction. Signal weights differ by person and device: time matters more on TV, likes and dislikes more on phones.
- TV viewers want longer videos and series, which is why those formats are recommended there, not because YouTube "prefers" long videos.
- Don't benchmark a new video against your best one, and keep one channel per audience. If the same people would watch both topics, one channel works; if not, separate channels make expectations clear.

**CTR is relative, not a target.** Beaupré's own example: would you rather have a video with a 20% click-through rate and 10,000 views, or 5% and 100,000 views? CTR falls as a video reaches colder audiences, so compare it with your own similar videos, not with a benchmark.

**Shorts run on a separate feed.** Each Short is shown to a small seed audience; reach widens when people watch and narrows when they swipe away. YouTube's Shorts lead summed it up as "the audience is the algorithm" ([OutlierKit's dated summary](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). Section 6 covers Shorts in full.

**Views changed meaning on August 24, 2026.** Public view counts now start at the first frame for every format. The older measure lives on as **Engaged views** in Analytics, and earnings, CTR, average view duration and retention still use it ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/), [Help: how engagement metrics are counted](https://support.google.com/youtubecreatorstudio/answer/2991785)). Compare old and new videos on engaged views, never on the public count.

## 2. Ideas and research — make what people already want

The idea sets most of a video's ceiling, so prove there is demand before you film. The most reliable evidence is an **outlier**: a video doing 5–100× its own channel's usual views, which shows the idea, not the channel's audience, drove the views.

**Pick an audience, not just a topic.** YouTube's guidance is to keep one channel per audience (section 1). Strategist Paddy Galloway, who has worked with MrBeast, Ryan Trahan and Red Bull, tells new channels to give it two years of consistent posting and not to stress about strategy and metrics before about 50 videos ([his thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/thread/1874513765762531734.html)). MrBeast's advice to small creators is similar: make 100 videos and improve one thing each time ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2342404)).

**The outlier method, step by step**

1. List 20–50 channels in your niche and next to it, ideally at your size or a little bigger.
2. Sort their videos by performance against each channel's own average, not by raw views. Ignore viral flukes with no repeatable idea.
3. For each outlier, write down the idea in one line, the title pattern and the thumbnail pattern.
4. Remix rather than copy: same proven desire, your angle, your evidence, your format.
5. Test the package (section 3) before you commit to production.

**Where ideas come from:** your own comments and community posts; search autocomplete; competitors' outliers; adjacent niches (an idea that worked in finance may work in fitness); and YouTube Studio itself. Studio announced **Research** and **Insights** destinations on September 23, 2026 to surface outlier videos and explain your past performance ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/new-tools-to-power-your-creation-journey-from-start-to-finish/)). A test version shows other channels' outlier multipliers and what your own viewers also watch ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/05/youtube-research-tab-outlier-multipliers-viewer-comparison-data/)). The older Inspiration tab is being phased out from August 2026 in favour of Ask Studio ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15575509)).

**Research tools** (prices from listing pages, November 2026; check before buying):

| Tool | Best for | Price |

| YouTube Studio Research / Insights | Outliers and your own audience's other channels, free | Free |
| [1of10](https://1of10.com/features/homepage-outliers) | Pure outlier research scored against each channel's average | Free plan; Basic about $19/mo |
| [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/features/outliers/) | Keywords, search demand, outliers, upload checklist | Free plan; Boost about $19/mo |

| TubeBuddy | Keyword research and browser-side tools | Free plan + paid tiers |

**A five-question idea filter.** Can it be told in one thumbnail and a title under about 60 characters? Is there outlier proof of demand? Can you make it better or different from what exists? Does it fit the audience you are building? Is it evergreen (views for years) or timely (views for days)? Plan both kinds, deliberately.

## 3. Packaging — titles and thumbnails

Package before you produce: write the title and design the thumbnail first, then make the video that keeps their promise. MrBeast's production team starts every video from its title and thumbnail ([Gigazine summary of the leaked guide](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)), and Paddy Galloway's teams draft 3 thumbnail options and 10 title options per video ([thread](https://threadreaderapp.com/user/PaddyG96)).

**What breakout thumbnails had in 2026.** vidIQ studied 500 breakout long-form videos across 30 niches published in the month to June 30, 2026 ([study](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-thumbnail-tips/)):

- 69% showed a human face, rising to 80% of the top 50.
- 56% used high-contrast colour, and 89% used a face or high contrast.
- Thumbnails with text used a median of five words.
- Only about 1 in 20 used an extreme expression such as shock, so you don't need the "shocked face".

**Rules that follow:** one clear subject; two or three elements at most; text that adds to the title instead of repeating it; check it at phone size and in both dark and light mode. Specs: 1280×720 (16:9), at least 640 px wide, under 2 MB, JPG, PNG or GIF.

**Titles:** one clear promise, front-loaded, readable in about 60 characters. Title and thumbnail should work as one unit: the thumbnail shows, the title tells. Clarity beats cleverness for anyone who doesn't know you yet.

**Promise, then deliver.** Packaging that over-promises gets clicks and then early exits, which hurts satisfaction. That is why YouTube judges its own A/B tests on watch time rather than CTR. As Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie put it, you have to click to watch, and you have to stay to build up time ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/renes-top-five-june-14-2024/)).

**Test & Compare (A/B testing), from the [Help Center](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400):**

- Test up to 3 titles, 3 thumbnails, or 3 title-and-thumbnail combinations; the winner is the one with the highest watch time share, and it is then shown to everyone.
- Results are Winner, Performed same, or Inconclusive. Options that differ more finish faster.
- It is desktop Studio only and not available for Shorts, or for made-for-kids, mature or private videos.
- Creators have run over 40 million experiments. Announced on September 23, 2026 without dates: A/B tests of up to 3 video cuts (to test hooks), dynamic thumbnails shown per audience segment, channel-matched thumbnail generation, and Ask Studio refreshing thumbnails on older videos ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/new-tools-to-power-your-creation-journey-from-start-to-finish/)).

**A packaging workflow:** write 10 titles → keep 3 → make 3 thumbnails that differ in concept, not just colour → test → months later, refresh the packaging on old videos that still get impressions.

## 4. Scripting, hooks and retention

The first 30 seconds decide most of a video's fate, and their job is to confirm that the video is what the title and thumbnail promised. YouTube's retention report measures exactly this: **Intro** is the percentage of viewers still watching after 30 seconds, and a high one means the opening matched the packaging ([Help: key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)).

**What YouTube's own report tells you to do:** if Intro is low, change the title and thumbnail to match the video, or try different openings; if the best moments come late, move them earlier, because audiences shrink over a video; and turn top moments and rewatch spikes into new videos. "Typical retention" compares a video with your last 10 of similar length ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1715160)).

**The hook (first 5–30 seconds)**

- Open on the payoff, the stakes or the most interesting moment, not on "Hey guys, welcome back". Then give only the context needed to follow it ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/pt/blog/post/audience-retention-secrets-youtube)).
- Say the promise back in the first sentences, so a viewer knows they clicked the right thing.
- Script your first lines word for word. MrBeast's guide calls the first minute the most important one, because that is when most people leave ([Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/?p=81151)).

**The body: a hook-deliver cycle, not a single hook.** Most creators stop hooking after the intro. Instead, open a small question, answer it, and open the next one, so there is always a reason to keep watching ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/tr/blog/post/audience-retention-secrets-youtube)).

**A structure that works for most talking or explainer videos**

1. Hook: payoff or stakes, plus the promise, in under 30 seconds.
2. Setup: why it matters and what we'll find out, in a few sentences.
3. Segments that escalate, each ending on a mini-hook into the next.
4. Payoff: the strongest part, delivered before people tire.
5. Fast ending: no long outro or recap. Point to one specific next video, then let the end screen do the work.

**Benchmarks (vidIQ, not official):** about 30% average percentage viewed is a realistic bar for long-form, with around 60% still watching at 30 seconds ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/tr/blog/post/increase-audience-retention-youtube/)). Your own trailing average is the real baseline.

**Writing for the ear:** short sentences; read every line aloud; cut anything the visuals already show; one idea per sentence. A test on the way: YouTube announced A/B tests of up to three video cuts, aimed at comparing hooks ([YouTube blog, Sep 23, 2026](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/new-tools-to-power-your-creation-journey-from-start-to-finish/)).

## 5. Production — filming, narration, sound, music, editing

Sound quality matters more than camera quality: viewers sit through an average picture but leave over bad audio. Spend first on a decent microphone, a quiet room and light; a modern phone is a good enough camera to start.

**Recording voice and narration**

- A dynamic or lavalier mic close to the mouth, in a room with soft furnishings, beats an expensive mic far away. Keep the same distance every session.
- Record with peaks around −6 dB and dialogue near −16 LUFS, which leaves headroom without sounding quiet ([Descript](https://descript.com/blog/article/how-to-set-the-perfect-audio-levels-for-video)).
- Read the script aloud before recording, record in short chunks, and redo any line that sounds read.

**Mix to YouTube's loudness.** YouTube plays everything at roughly −14 LUFS integrated loudness. It turns louder mixes down but does not turn quieter ones up, so a quiet mix sounds weak next to other videos ([Hollyland](https://store.hollyland.com/blogs/creator-hub/normalize-audio-on-youtube)). Master to about −14 LUFS with true peaks around −1 dB, using any loudness meter (DaVinci Resolve Fairlight, Audition, Audacity's Loudness Normalization, Descript).

**Music without copyright trouble**

- **YouTube Audio Library** (Studio → Audio Library): music and sound effects that won't be claimed through Content ID and can be monetised. YouTube says only Audio Library tracks are known to it to be copyright-safe ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/3376882)).
- "Royalty-free" music from elsewhere can still be claimed. Writing "free song" in your description doesn't stop Content ID ([Help: tips to find safe music](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15577610)).
- **Creator Music** (US YPP creators only so far): license mainstream tracks up front, or share the video's revenue with the rights holder. Some licenses expire, and Shorts and live streams aren't eligible ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11610609)).
- Paid libraries such as Epidemic Sound, Artlist or Musicbed clear claims through your account. Keep your license records.

**Other languages, from the same video.** Auto dubbing now covers 27 languages, 8 of them with Expressive Speech that keeps your tone, and you can upload your own dubs instead ([YouTube blog, May 2026](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-auto-dubbing-explained/)). In the multi-language audio pilot, creators got over 25% of their watch time from non-primary languages, and Jamie Oliver's views tripled ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3044901)). Start with one evergreen video and compare using the audio-track filter in Analytics.

**Editing for retention:** cut every pause and repeated line; change something on screen every few seconds in fast formats (B-roll, zoom, graphic, text); use sound effects and music changes to mark new segments; keep the frame clean for mobile. Free tools are enough: DaVinci Resolve, CapCut and the YouTube Create app, which now has a Gemini editing assistant for Shorts ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/new-tools-to-power-your-creation-journey-from-start-to-finish/)). Descript lets you edit by editing the transcript, which suits narrated videos.

**Filming basics:** a big soft light in front of you at about 45°, the camera at eye level, the lens near your eyeline when talking to camera, and a background with some depth. Shoot 4K if you can, so you can punch in during the edit.

## 6. Shorts playbook

Shorts are discovery: a cheap way to reach people who don't know you, which works best when it leads them to long-form videos. Since October 15, 2024 a Short is any vertical or square video up to three minutes ([OutlierKit's dated list](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)).

**How the Shorts feed decides.** Viewers don't click a thumbnail; they decide in the first second whether to keep watching or swipe. Each Short goes to a small seed audience and spreads only if they watch. Studio reports this as **Shown in feed** and **How many chose to view** (the share who viewed rather than swiped away). YouTube's Shorts lead said there is no ideal length, only the length the story needs, and warned that deleting and re-posting a Short can be treated as spam ([SEJ](https://searchenginejournal.com/youtube-explains-how-shorts-algorithm-works/494953), [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). Since October 1, 2026 the feed reduces reach for content re-uploaded from other creators.

**A useful benchmark.** Paddy Galloway's analysis of 3.3 billion Shorts views found the best performers had a 70–90% "viewed" rate ([Vaizle summary](https://insights.vaizle.com/how-youtube-shorts-algorithm-works/)).

**Making Shorts that get watched**

- The first frame is the hook: start mid-action or on the most surprising line. No logo, no greeting.
- One complete idea per Short: hook, middle, payoff. "Part 3 of 7" rarely works.
- Big on-screen captions inside the vertical safe area, away from the buttons on the right and the text at the bottom.
- End so the loop feels natural; a replay counts as more watch time.
- Post Shorts that suit the same audience as your long-form videos, or you'll build an audience that never watches your main work.

**Turn Shorts viewers into long-form viewers.** Shorts can't have end screens, and external links in Shorts descriptions and comments were removed in 2023 ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2023/09/01/youtube-related-links-multi-format-creators-shorts-live-long-form/amp/)). Use the **Related video** link on every Short, pointing to the full video, and point to it on screen ([YouTube's guide](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)).

**How Shorts pay** ([Help: Shorts monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220)):

1. Ad revenue from the Shorts feed is pooled each month, by country.
2. Part of the pool pays for music: a Short with one licensed track sends half its share to music costs; two tracks send two-thirds.
3. The remainder is split by each creator's share of engaged Shorts views.
4. You keep 45% of your allocation, whether or not you used music.

Shorts over one minute that contain claimed content are blocked. Shorts earn far less per view than long-form, and from February 1, 2027 Shorts revenue sharing needs 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, even for existing partners (section 10).

## 7. Faceless and AI-assisted channels

Faceless channels work, and AI tools are allowed. What YouTube refuses to pay for is content that is mass-produced, repetitive or reused without real added value. Lofi Girl, the Lockpicking Lawyer and Daily Dose of Internet grew without making a face the centre of the content ([Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com/resources/blog/faceless-youtube-channel)). Their personality comes through the script, the voice, the editing and the point of view.

**The monetisation rules, from YouTube's [channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392):**

| Policy | Allowed | Not allowed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic or repetitive content | The same intro and outro with different content in between; a series where each video has its own storyline or focus | Low-variation videos with little educational value; characters in the same situation with the same outcome; image slideshows, templated stories or scrolling text with little narrative; AI content from generic templates |
| Reused content (judged across the whole channel, even with the owner's permission) | Clips used in a critical review; reactions that comment on the original; footage re-edited with a new storyline and commentary | Show clips stitched together with little narrative; song compilations; downloaded content without real changes; channels that only read out others' material, such as news text |
| Unsatisfying or off-putting content | A coherent story; AI used to visualise a character and story you invented, to edit scripts or to make background visuals | Repetitive disturbing themes; stitched AI clips with no logical progression; deceptive images such as a fake celebrity death |
| AI personas | AI as a creative tool serving your own vision | AI personas presenting as human experts on health, legal, financial or political topics |

The "inauthentic content" name dates from July 15, 2025, when the old "repetitious content" policy was renamed and clarified. In July 2026 YouTube spelled out the categories above and said it was not a new policy ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/07/13/youtube-inauthentic-content-monetization-policy-update/)).

**Disclose realistic AI.** YouTube requires you to disclose realistic content that is generated or meaningfully altered with AI, in the upload flow's AI-use setting. Unrealistic content and minor edits don't need it ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Since May 2026 YouTube also uses its own signals to detect AI content, and shows a more prominent label for photorealistic AI ([YouTube blog](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/improving-ai-labels-for-viewers-and-creators/)).

**A faceless model that stays monetisable**

- Your own research and angle on every video, so no two videos are interchangeable.
- Your own script, and a consistent narrator: your voice, a hired voice, or a clearly disclosed synthetic voice.
- Visuals that carry the story: diagrams, maps, footage you shot or licensed, and AI images only where they serve the narrative.
- A self-test: watch three of your videos with the sound off. If you can't tell them apart, the channel looks templated.

**Enforcement is real.** Reports say YouTube removed or wiped 16 channels with a combined 35 million subscribers under this policy in January 2026 ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm)). A demonetised channel loses its ad income, so build original value from video one.

## 8. Uploading and discovery settings

Metadata won't rescue a weak video, but it helps the right viewers find a good one and keeps them watching your other videos.

**Title and description.** Put the words people would search for near the start of the title, but keep the title written for a human (section 3). The first two lines of the description show before "more", so use them to say what the viewer gets. Tags matter little; YouTube says they mainly help with common misspellings.

**Chapters** ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)). Add timestamps in the description: the first must be 00:00, list at least three in ascending order, and make each chapter at least 10 seconds long. Chapter titles can show in search and make long videos easier to navigate. If you don't add them, YouTube may create automatic chapters, which you can switch off.

**End screens and cards.** End screens can sit in the last 5–20 seconds of a video that is at least 25 seconds long, with up to four elements on a 16:9 video ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789)). They aren't available on videos set as made for kids. Point the end screen at one specific next video, and say why on screen; "best for viewer" lets YouTube pick. Cards can appear at any point; use them where a viewer might want more on something you just mentioned, not in the first minute.

**Playlists and series.** Group videos a viewer would watch in order or in a binge. A playlist can feed session watch time, and on TV, where long videos and series do well, it gives viewers an easy next step.

**Captions and languages.** Upload accurate captions or correct the automatic ones; they help viewers who watch muted, help accessibility and feed search. Add translated titles and descriptions for big non-native audiences, and consider auto dubbing or your own multi-language audio tracks (section 5).

**Schedule and consistency.** Publish when your audience is online (Analytics → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube"). Keep a pace you can sustain without dropping quality; YouTube recommends videos to viewers, not channels on a schedule, so a better video beats an on-time one.

**Hype.** Viewers can hype up to three long-form videos a week, if the video is less than seven days old and the channel is in the YouTube Partner Program with 500–500,000 subscribers. Smaller channels get more bonus points per hype, and the most-hyped videos appear on a weekly leaderboard ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15333273)). Ask your core fans to hype new uploads in the first days.

**Upload checklist**

1. Title and thumbnail chosen (and Test & Compare set up with up to three thumbnails or titles).
2. Description: two-line summary, chapters, links, disclosures for sponsors or AI.
3. Audience setting: made for kids or not (section 12).
4. AI-content disclosure where needed (section 7); paid promotion box if sponsored.
5. End screen, cards, playlist, captions, Related video link on the matching Short.
6. Schedule for when your audience is online; reply to comments in the first hour.

## 9. Analytics: what to read and what to do about it

YouTube Studio's Analytics answers three questions: did people choose the video, did they stay, and did they come back. Compare each video against your own channel's typical results, not against other channels; YouTube itself says CTR naturally falls as a video reaches wider, colder audiences (section 1).

| Question | Where to look | What a problem looks like | What to change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Did they choose it? | Impressions, impressions click-through rate, traffic sources | Many impressions, few views compared with your typical video | Packaging: title, thumbnail, the idea itself (section 3) |
| Did they stay? | Average view duration, average percentage viewed, audience retention graph, key moments | A steep drop in the first 30 seconds, or a dip at the same kind of moment in several videos | Hook, pacing, cutting slow parts (section 4) |
| Did they come back? | Returning viewers, subscribers gained per video, end screen click rate | Views come only from new viewers, few return | Series, playlists, end screens, community (sections 8 and 11) |
| Who are they? | Audience tab: when viewers are on YouTube, other channels and videos they watch, formats they watch | Your audience watches very different things from what you make | Ideas that bridge what they already watch (section 2) |
| Is it Shorts or long-form? | Content tab split by Videos, Shorts, Live | Shorts swiped away a lot; long-form fine (or the reverse) | Treat them as separate products |

**Retention graph reading.** The audience retention report marks **key moments**: the intro (how many are still watching after 30 seconds), spikes (parts people rewatch or share), dips (parts they skip) and top moments. Copy what the spikes do; cut or tighten what sits before each dip.

**The first hours and the long tail.** Look at a video's performance against your typical range after 24 hours and again after a week; YouTube says some videos find their audience late. Don't delete underperforming long-form videos; change the title or thumbnail once you have enough data.

**Research and Ask Studio.** The Research tab shows what your audience and viewers in general are searching for, including content gaps where searches find few good videos. Ask Studio, YouTube's AI assistant in Studio, answers questions about your channel's data in plain language and gives feedback on ideas; check its answers against the actual reports before acting.

**A weekly review routine (30 minutes)**

1. List last week's uploads with impressions, CTR, average view duration and views versus your typical range.
2. For the best and worst video, note one cause each: packaging, hook, topic or timing.
3. Open the retention graph of the best video; write down what happens at its spikes.
4. Pick one change for next week's video and record it, so you can tell later whether it worked.

## 10. Monetisation

Ad revenue is usually the smallest part of a mature channel's income. Plan for several income streams, and build the audience first: every stream below depends on people who trust you.

**YouTube Partner Program tiers** ([Help: how to earn money](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72857), [expanded YPP](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13429240))

| Tier | Requirements (today) | What it unlocks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fan funding (in available countries) | 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in the last 90 days, and either 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days; age 18+ for most features | Channel memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers, Super Thanks, Shopping (own products and the affiliate program) |
| Full ad revenue | 1,000 subscribers and either 4,000 public watch hours on long-form in 12 months or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days | Watch-page ads, Shorts feed ads, YouTube Premium revenue |

**The 2027 change.** YouTube announced on August 10, 2026 that from **February 1, 2027** new creators need 8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days to join for ad revenue; channels already in the program stay in. From the same date, Shorts revenue sharing needs 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, even for existing partners, while long-form earnings continue below that. YouTube also announced Shopping bonuses, brand-deal incentives and trend boosts ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)).

**How the money is split**

- Long-form watch-page ads: you get 55% of net ad revenue.
- Shorts feed ads: you get 45% of your allocation from the pool (section 6).
- YouTube Premium: you get a share of subscription revenue based on how much Premium members watch you.
- Memberships, Super Chat, Super Stickers and Super Thanks: you get 70% after taxes and app-store fees.

**RPM versus CPM.** CPM is what advertisers pay per 1,000 ad impressions; RPM is what you earn per 1,000 views after YouTube's share and after views with no ads. RPM is the number to plan with. It varies widely by topic (finance and software pay far more than entertainment), by viewer country, by video length (videos of 8 minutes or more can carry mid-roll ads) and by season (Q4 ad budgets are high, January is low).

**Other income streams**

| Stream | How it works | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Channel memberships | Monthly paid tiers with perks: badges, emoji, members-only videos or posts | A loyal audience that wants more of you |
| Super Thanks, Super Chat | One-off tips on videos and live chats | Any channel with engaged fans; mention it lightly |
| YouTube Shopping affiliate | Tag other brands' products and earn commission; from March 2026 open to YPP channels with 500+ subscribers in supported countries ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-shopping-expansion-500-subscribers/)) | Reviews, tutorials, gear, beauty, tech |
| Your own products | Connect a store (such as Shopify) and show products under videos | Merch, courses, templates, physical goods |
| Sponsorships | Brands pay for an integration; YouTube BrandConnect matches creators and brands | Once you have a clear, consistent audience; always tick "includes paid promotion" |
| External affiliate links | Commission links in the description | Anything people buy after watching |
| Services and licensing | Consulting, freelance, licensing your footage | Expertise channels and skilled filmmakers |

**Sponsor basics.** Price from your typical views per video and audience fit, not subscriber count. Keep the read honest and short, place it after you've delivered value (often after the hook), and never let a sponsor change what you say about their product.

## 11. Growth and community

**Pick one audience per channel.** YouTube recommends videos to people based on what similar viewers watched (section 1). A channel that mixes unrelated topics confuses that signal and its own subscribers; start a second channel for a different audience instead.

**The stages most channels go through**

| Stage | Goal | What to focus on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to first 1,000 subscribers | Find one idea that works | Volume with learning: publish, review each video (section 9), keep what worked. Paddy Galloway's rough rule is about two years or 50 videos before judging a channel |
| 1,000 to 10,000 | Repeat what worked | Turn hits into series and formats; raise packaging and production quality |
| 10,000 to 100,000 | Build a team and systems | Hire an editor or thumbnail designer; batch scripting and filming; add income streams (section 10) |
| 100,000+ | Expand without losing the core | New formats or spin-off channels, sponsors, products, other languages |

**Comments are a growth tool.** Reply in the first hour after publishing, heart and pin a comment that starts a conversation, and mine comments for next video ideas. Turn on the AI comment moderation tools if spam is a problem, but read the held-for-review queue.

**Posts (the Community tab).** Use polls, images and short text between uploads to keep subscribers engaged and to test ideas: a poll between two topics is a cheap check of which one your audience wants.

**Collaborations.** Work with creators whose audience overlaps yours but who aren't direct rivals. A good collaboration gives both audiences a reason to watch, not just a shout-out.

**Off-platform.** An email list is the one audience you own; offer something useful (a guide, a template) in exchange. Clips on other platforms can send viewers, but post each one natively and don't cut the long video into fragments YouTube sees as duplicates.

**Burnout is the most common reason channels stop.** Choose a schedule you can keep for years, batch work, and plan breaks. YouTube says a short break doesn't permanently hurt a channel, because recommendations follow each video's performance with viewers.

## 12. Policies and risks

One policy mistake can undo years of work. Read the rules once, then check new kinds of videos against them before publishing. This section summarises YouTube's rules; it isn't legal advice.

**Two strike systems** ([Strikes FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9235777))

|  | Community Guidelines | Copyright |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First problem | Usually a warning; it expires after 90 days if you take the optional policy training and don't break the same policy again | A strike once a rights holder sends a valid legal takedown request; first strike requires Copyright School |
| Each strike | First strike: no uploads, live streams or posts for one week; second within 90 days: two weeks | Video removed; strike expires after 90 days |
| Three strikes in 90 days | Channel can be terminated | Account and associated channels can be terminated |
| Deleting the video | Doesn't remove the strike | Doesn't remove the strike |

Sources: [Community Guidelines strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802032), [copyright strikes](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2814000).

**Content ID claims are not strikes.** When Content ID matches someone's music or footage in your video, the rights holder can block it, track it or take its ad revenue, but your channel isn't penalised. You can dispute a claim you believe is wrong (for example, your own licence), or remove or replace the claimed part in Studio. Shorts over one minute with an active Content ID claim are blocked.

**Music.** Use the YouTube Audio Library, a licence from a stock-music service that covers YouTube, or music you own. "No copyright intended" and crediting the artist don't give you any rights.

**Fair use** is a legal doctrine in some countries that can allow limited use of copyrighted material for purposes such as commentary, criticism or education. It is decided case by case, YouTube can't decide it for you, and other countries have different rules. Commentary on short clips is the common safer pattern; re-uploading other people's work is not.

**Made for kids.** Every creator must set whether each video or the channel is made for kids, under the US Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). Videos made for kids get no personalised ads and lose features such as comments, end screens and cards, notifications, the miniplayer and Super Chat; made-for-kids channels also lose memberships and posts ([Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654)). Set it honestly: YouTube can override the setting, and regulators can fine creators.

**Other rules that catch growing channels**

- **Misleading metadata:** thumbnails or titles that show or promise something the video doesn't contain can be removed under the spam and deceptive practices policy.
- **Inauthentic and reused content:** affects monetisation across the whole channel (section 7).
- **Paid promotion:** tick the paid-promotion box and say so in the video whenever a brand paid or gave you something.
- **Realistic AI:** disclose it in the upload flow (section 7).
- **Fake engagement:** bought views, subscribers or comments break the rules and can get videos removed or the channel terminated. Sub-for-sub schemes count too.
- **Advertiser-friendly guidelines:** strong swearing in the first seconds, violence, or sensitive topics can limit ads on a video (the yellow icon) without breaking Community Guidelines. Ask for human review if you disagree.

## 13. Agents and Claude for YouTube

An AI agent can do most of the research, drafting and admin around a channel. It can't make the video worth watching: the idea, the judgement and the voice are still yours. This section is written so an agent (or a person directing one) can act on it.

**The official APIs**

| API | What it does | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [YouTube Data API v3](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3) | Search, read public video and channel data, upload and update videos, playlists, thumbnails, captions, comments | Default quota: 10,000 units a day for most calls, plus separate buckets of 100 `search.list` calls and 100 `videos.insert` uploads a day ([quota page](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/determine_quota_cost), updated November 12, 2026). Reading data costs 1 unit; most writes cost 50; captions upload 400. More quota needs a compliance audit |
| [YouTube Analytics API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/analytics) | Your channel's views, watch time, retention, traffic sources, demographics, revenue (if you own the channel) | OAuth sign-in by the channel owner; read-only |
| [YouTube Reporting API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/reporting) | Bulk daily CSV reports of the same data | For dashboards and long-term history |

Uploads through the API from unverified API projects are locked as private until the project passes an audit, so test with a private video first.

**MCP servers.** Several community MCP servers wrap these APIs so Claude can call them as tools; examples include [ZubeidHendricks/youtube-mcp-server](https://github.com/ZubeidHendricks/youtube-mcp-server) (Data API, transcripts) and a hosted, paid option, [Zernio](https://zernio.com/youtube-mcp) (uploads and analytics). They are third-party code: read the source, give them the smallest OAuth scope that works, and keep credentials out of shared config. Transcript tools that scrape YouTube's pages rather than using the API can break without notice and may conflict with YouTube's terms.

**Pipelines that work well**

| Job | What the agent does | What stays human |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outlier research | Pulls recent videos from 20–50 channels in your niche, ranks them by views compared with each channel's median, groups the outliers by idea | Choosing which idea fits you |
| Title and thumbnail ideas | Writes 10+ titles per idea, briefs for 3 thumbnails, checks they match the video | The final pick; Test & Compare decides between the top 3 |
| Script support | Research brief with sources, outline, hook options, fact-check of your draft | Your script, your voice, your claims |
| Publishing admin | Chapters from the transcript, description, translated titles, end-screen and playlist checklist (section 8) | Reviewing every field before publishing |
| Weekly analytics report | Runs the review routine in section 9 through the Analytics API and writes a short report | The one change to make next week |
| Comment triage | Groups comments into questions, ideas and problems; drafts replies | Sending replies; anything sensitive |
| Repurposing | Finds 30–60 second moments for Shorts from the retention graph and transcript | Editing and checking they stand alone |

**Rules for an agent working on a channel**

1. Never publish, delete or change monetisation settings without the owner's explicit approval; prefer drafts, private uploads and scheduled videos.
2. Never generate fake engagement, mass-produce near-identical videos, or impersonate a real person (sections 7 and 12).
3. Disclose realistic AI-generated content; mark paid promotion.
4. Cite a source for every factual claim that goes into a script.
5. Track API quota and stop before it runs out; uploads are capped at 100 a day by default.

**Automating it.** To run any of these on a schedule or with hooks and subagents in Claude Code, see the companion [Claude automation directory](https://claude.ai/artifact/H6idk1MoA39ynYkapdgwUz).

## 14. Standout cases and what to copy

| Case | What happened | The lesson |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MrBeast's production guide | His internal guide for his team, made public in 2024, treats CTR, average view duration and average percentage viewed as the core metrics, and says the title and thumbnail are decided before the video is made | Design the click and the first minute first; the rest of the video has to keep the promise |
| Paddy Galloway's Shorts study | An analysis of 3.3 billion Shorts views found the best performers had 70–90% of viewers choose to watch rather than swipe ([Vaizle summary](https://insights.vaizle.com/how-youtube-shorts-algorithm-works/)) | In Shorts, the first second decides everything |
| Jamie Oliver and multi-language audio | In YouTube's multi-language audio pilot, Jamie Oliver's channel tripled its views after adding dubbed tracks; pilot creators got over 25% of watch time from non-primary languages | Translation is a growth lever once a format works |
| Lofi Girl, Lockpicking Lawyer, Daily Dose of Internet | Large channels with no face at the centre of the content ([Storyblocks](https://www.storyblocks.com/resources/blog/faceless-youtube-channel)) | A faceless channel needs a strong identity: a mood, a skill, a voice or a curator's taste |
| The January 2026 AI-slop terminations | Reports say 16 channels with 35 million subscribers were removed or wiped under the inauthentic content policy ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/youtubes-ai-slop-crackdown-cant-tell-a-directed-ai-film-from-a-bot-farm)) | Mass-produced AI videos can grow fast and disappear overnight |
| Todd Beaupré on breaks | YouTube's discovery lead said the algorithm focuses on individual videos and doesn't punish a channel for a break or a weak video ([SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-algorithm-myths-debunked-insights-from-the-growth-team/510091/)) | Make each video as good as it can be; you don't need to upload while burnt out |

**A pattern across almost every success:** a narrow audience to start, one format repeated and improved many times, packaging designed before production, and a creator who studies their own analytics every week.
